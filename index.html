Research direction

From observers to trusted agents: reliable, intelligent decision-making in space systems

I want to pursue a PhD at the intersection of autonomous space systems and trustworthy AI. I am interested in investigating what agentic AI frameworks can bring to the space domain under strict energy and memory constraints. The work starts from a single space system and scales to multi-agent systems.

Onboard perception

Real-time, AI-based decision-making on energy-efficient space hardware, for space robotics, Earth observation and deep-space missions.

Autonomous and distributed systems

Beyond perception: agentic AI for subsystem orchestration and distributed computing across a constellation or swarm.

Trustworthy space AI

Reliable AI for critical operations, such as assisting astronauts or commanding a spacecraft, which is still a largely open field.

Highlighted projects

Three projects that show how I work, from problem to flight hardware.

2023 – 2026, with TU Delft, Ubotica and ESA Φ-Lab

Onboard thermal hotspot segmentation

Problem. Detecting thermal anomalies such as wildfires and volcanic activity quickly requires processing imagery in orbit. Conventional pipelines depend on ground-processed products, which adds hours of latency.

Contribution. I designed the first CNN-based onboard payload pipeline for real-time thermal hotspot segmentation in raw multispectral imagery. I developed automatic labelling to produce the first segmentation dataset of raw Sentinel-2 hotspots, contributed to THRawS, and verified the full pipeline on CubeSat-class hardware, measuring power draw and inference latency.

Hardware
CogniSAT-XE2, Raspberry Pi 3B+
Supervision
J. Guo, D. Rijlaarsdam, G. Meoni, R. Del Prete
Output
Journal article, JAG 2026

2024 – 2025, with NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory and Ubotica

Dynamic Targeting

Problem. Dynamic Targeting lets a satellite analyse imagery on board and re-plan its own observations in orbit, for example to avoid clouds or follow a feature of interest, without waiting for commands from the ground.

Contribution. In collaboration with NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, I contributed to satellite operations and image analysis on the CogniSAT-6/HAMMER mission, the in-orbit platform used to demonstrate the concept.

Platform
CogniSAT-6 / HAMMER
Partners
NASA JPL, Ubotica
Output
Conference paper, ASTRA 2025

2026 – present, ETH Zurich Automatic Control Laboratory

Agentic AI onboard spacecraft simulator

Problem. In coordinated constellations, an acquisition failure on the upstream satellite cascades downstream before the ground segment can intervene. Rule-based automation covers only the failures defined in advance.

Contribution. I am building a simulator in which an onboard LLM agent orchestrates the full acquisition pipeline, calling satellite subsystem functions as tools, and coordinates tasking with a second satellite through natural-language messages over an inter-satellite link.

Result. Running a small 4-bit LLM, the agent was as reliable as rule-based automation on known failures, and also handled situations the rules never anticipated.
